Hello!

My application is on flathub as: ZQuest Classic | Flathub

As of today, there is a “Potentially Unsafe” warning with this:

Can access hardware devices; Can read/write your downloads; Uses a legacy windowing system

I’m well aware that the permissions model protects users and this is to alert users that they are opting into a permission. My concern is how non-technical users view this - I’ve had a few reports from users in my community that upon seeing this notice they think best not to install the app (this is after failing to manually install it themselves via a source distribution, which of course they can’t manage being non-techincal). So the main usecase (in my view) of flathub for our project - being a great, simple method for anyone to install our app - is harmed.

Going through the specific permissions:

  1. “Can access hardware devices” - we support gamepads. As far as I can tell, there is no more-granular permission to request.

  2. “Can read/write downloads” - We could probably change this to just reading; but the reason for this is to make downloading assets from an external site simpler to load into the program (asking a user to move files to the right place would be a headache)

  3. The legacy windowing thing - yeah, that’s something we should change. No problems with this warning.

Ideally, flatpak would support some more granular perms for the gamepad usecase; but what would really help is if we could provide a “justification” for each permission that an app requires in the flatpak config. As it is today, the current messaging around “unsafe apps” is probably a little more user unfriendly than it needs to be, and being able to explain each permission needed (right alongside its warning) may help.

Sorry for the topic bump (it seemed the “most recent” I could find on the matter), but still relevant and wanted to add updated feedback, particularly for fellow linux newbies.

Brand new linux user here, about 3 weeks in. I have zero idea how this stuff works, self teaching atm after leaving the apple microsoft ecosystems. Like I only learned 2 weeks ago that an OS and the desktop can be separate things lol. SO imagine how good I am at understanding the “unsafe” designation.

The “unsafe” designation definitely had a direct negative impact on my adoption of linux or open source offerings initially. As I looked around Flathub for various options, almost every single one had an “unsafe” designation. When I read the list of what was causing that designation, I don’t know enough to know how serious to take any of the warnings.

Worst yet was a majority of the open source apps I wanted to use - apps that have a fairly high rating among open source advocates - had this “unsafe” designation. Even really reputable ones. Meanwhile the “verified” proprietary apps are by companies who quietly sell my PII data or scrape my content to train their LLMs without my consent. In this context the “unsafe” and “verified” designations didn’t give me any actionable end user feedback to avoid software with predatory intentions. It just made the Flathub seem unsafe. Thankfully when literally every single solitary app that I tried after a few hours was coming up “unsafe”, I figured that it had to be a display bug or something and not actually unsafe. A less persistent end user than me though would have just noped out and gone back to what they’re used to instead of giving this a real try.

I hope someone address it and makes the feedback more meaningful for end users. What an app is capable of doing, and intuiting the intentions of the designers, are two different things. It’s the UI equivalent of labeling the kitchen drawer with “DANGEROUS WEAPON INSIDE” because it has a steak knife in it. Like um yeah, someone could in fact hurt someone or themself with a steak knife. But most ppl are just gonna use it to eat a steak, so how useful or informative is such a label in that context?
